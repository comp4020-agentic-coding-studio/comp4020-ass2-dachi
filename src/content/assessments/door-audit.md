## The brief

> Find ten doors. For each one, say what it wants you to do, what you actually
> did the first time, and whether those were the same thing.

A door audit is not a complaint file. Anyone can list ten doors that are
annoying; the studio is a semester on why, and "why" needs categories that
survive contact with the next building. Use whatever vocabulary you've picked
up from the hardware clinic and lectures — push plate versus pull handle,
signifier versus affordance, the difference between a door that's wrong and
one that's merely unfamiliar — and apply it the same way across all ten, so a
reader can compare door three against door seven and trust that the standard
didn't move.

Doors that work are part of the assignment too. A survey that finds nothing
but failure has usually stopped looking, or picked its ten doors to make a
point rather than to test one.

## What you submit

A written audit, one entry per door: a photo or sketch, the location, and your
judgement against the criterion you're applying. Ten to fifteen hundred words
total is enough if the categories are doing real work; padding a thin argument
with more doors doesn't help.

Bring the doors you're least sure about to the week 4 studio crit before you
submit — that's what the session is for.
