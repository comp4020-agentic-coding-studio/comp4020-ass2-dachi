## The brief

> Turn your semester's doors into a field guide: a typology someone else could
> use to look at a door they've never seen and know what to check.

This is not a portfolio of your other three assessments. A field guide has its
own job: take everything the audit, the redesign and the prototype taught you
about how doors succeed and fail, and compress it into categories that
generalise past the specific doors you happened to study. The door you
redesigned belongs in the typology like any other example, not bolted on at
the end as "and here's what I built."

The test for whether it's working: hand it to someone who has never taken
Doorology, point them at a door on the other side of campus, and see whether
the guide tells them what to look for. If it only makes sense next to the
doors you personally photographed, it's still a diary, not a field guide.

## What you submit

An illustrated document — the categories, worked through with examples drawn
from your semester's doors, including the redesigned one. Length is whatever
the typology needs; padding it to look thorough is easy to spot and doesn't
help. There is no separate mechanism for demonstrating process here — the
Field Guide is judged as a finished artefact in its own right.
