A door is small enough to hold in your head completely and common enough that
you've operated a few hundred of them without ever being taught how. That gap
— total familiarity, zero formal attention — is the whole course. Twelve
weeks, one object, four pieces of graded work that each ask you to look
harder than the last: an audit of ten real doors, a redesign of one, a
prototype of the fix, and a field guide that turns the semester into
something a stranger could use.

The studio format matters more than the topic. You will spend more of this
course standing in front of doors than reading about them, and more time in
crits than in lectures — this is one of only six lectures all semester, and
the rest of the timetable is studio.

## Outline

- why doors, specifically, and not "everyday design" in general
- the shape of the semester: audit, redesign, prototype, field guide
- what a studio crit expects from you that a lecture doesn't
