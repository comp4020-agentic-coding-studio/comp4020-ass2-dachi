A redesign proposal is a document; a prototype is the first version of the
door that someone other than you can operate. That jump is bigger than it
looks, and the method you pick decides whether it happens at all. A
full-size cardboard mockup proves whether a signage change reads at a glance.
A laser-cut panel proves whether wording fits where you think it does. A
foamcore model at reduced scale proves a hardware layout without needing a
real door to test it on. None of these prove everything, and picking one
means being honest about what it leaves unproven — which is exactly what the
prototype and crit assessment later asks you to say out loud.

The wrong move is choosing the method that's easiest to build rather than the
one that tests the actual claim your redesign makes. If your fix lives or
dies on whether the hardware feels right in the hand, a drawing doesn't test
it, however good the drawing is.
