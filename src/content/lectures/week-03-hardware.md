A flat plate tells you to push. A handle tells you to pull, or turn, or lift
— which one depends on its shape, and a badly shaped handle can tell you the
wrong thing as confidently as a well shaped one tells you the right thing.
This is the vocabulary the "Norman door" made famous: a door's affordance is
what it actually lets you do; its signifier is what it tells you to do; the
two agreeing is the exception worth noticing, not the rule.

Hardware is where this becomes concrete rather than philosophical. A closer's
spring force and a hinge's swing direction decide what's physically possible;
a plate, a handle, a sign, and their placement decide what a stranger will
try first. The audit asks you to judge ten doors against a stated criterion —
this lecture is where that criterion comes from.
