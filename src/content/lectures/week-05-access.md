Most doors are designed, tested and approved by people who can see the sign,
reach the handle, push the weight, and clear the threshold without noticing
any of it as an achievement. An accessibility review exists because that
experience isn't universal, and a door that clears every check a sighted,
mobile, unhurried reviewer would think to run can still fail someone else
completely.

The recurring failures aren't exotic: a door heavier than it needs to be, a
clear opening narrower than a wheelchair or a pram, an automatic opener timed
for someone who walks faster than you do, a threshold lip that's invisible
until a wheel catches on it. None of these show up in a glance. All of them
show up the moment you ask "who is this door built for by default, and who
wasn't in the room when that default was set."
