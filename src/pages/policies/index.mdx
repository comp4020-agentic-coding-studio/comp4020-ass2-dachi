# Policies and support

## Late work and extensions

The door audit, redesign proposal and prototype are each due at the studio
session that marks them — the audit crit, the redesign crit, or the public
crit. The field guide is the one exception: it's due at the end of the
teaching period, eleven days after the closing crit, so your typology can
absorb what that last session's wider survey of doors turns up. Work
submitted after the relevant deadline loses 5% of the assessment's weight per
day late, to a maximum of five days, after which it is marked as submitted
with that penalty applied regardless of quality.

Extensions are granted for the usual reasons (illness, misadventure) and are
asked for before the due date wherever possible, not after. Ask Petra
directly rather than waiting for the studio session to raise it.

## Using AI on this course's work

Doorology's assessments are built on things you did with your own hands and
eyes: doors you actually walked up to, hardware you actually adjusted,
prototypes someone else actually tested. AI tools are fine for drafting
prose, checking your writing, or looking up a general concept from a
lecture — the same way they'd be fine for any writing task. They cannot audit
a door, adjust a closer, or test a prototype on a stranger for you. If a
door audit entry, a redesign specification, or a crit defence describes an
observation you didn't make or a test you didn't run, that's a fabrication
problem regardless of how it was produced, not a tooling problem — say what
you actually did, plainly, and cite an AI tool the same way you'd cite any
other source that shaped your prose.

## Handling hardware safely

The hardware clinic (week 3) and making clinic (week 9) put real door
hardware — hinges, closers, latches, panic bars — in your hands, some of it
under spring tension. Callum demonstrates each part's safe handling before
anyone touches it; follow his lead specifically for closers, whose adjustment
screws release stored force if turned the wrong way. If you're prototyping
with tools of your own (a blade, a drill, anything with a blade or a motor),
use them only in the making clinic or with a tutor present, not unsupervised
in your own time.

## Getting help

For anything about the course as a whole, email Petra. For a specific
hardware or access question, ask Callum during a studio or clinic session
first — most of these questions are faster answered with the door in front
of you than over email.
